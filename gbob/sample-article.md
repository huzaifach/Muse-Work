Most plumbing disasters do not start with a bang. They start with a slow drip under the sink, a toilet that runs a little too long, or a water heater nobody has looked at in six years. By the time water is pooling on the floor, the repair bill is often ten times what prevention would have cost.

The good news: residential plumbing is one of the most forgiving systems in your home — if you give it a little attention. None of the habits below require special tools or professional training. Together, they take less than two hours a year.

## 1. Look under your sinks twice a year

Cabinet bases hide the earliest warning signs: damp spots, warped wood, a musty smell, or green corrosion on supply valves. Put a reminder on your calendar for spring and fall. Run your hand along the P-trap and supply lines while the faucet runs. If anything feels damp, tighten the slip nuts by hand — a quarter turn is often enough — and recheck in a week. Catching a slow leak here can save you from replacing an entire vanity.

## 2. Know where your main water shutoff is — and test it

In an emergency, the difference between a mop-up and a renovation is how fast you can stop the water. Find your main shutoff valve today (usually near the water meter, in the garage, or in a utility closet). Turn it off and on once a year so it does not seize up. Show every adult in the house where it is. This single habit has saved more homes from catastrophic damage than any gadget on the market.

## 3. Flush your water heater annually

Sediment builds up at the bottom of every tank-style water heater, forcing the burner or element to work harder and shortening the tank's life. Draining a few gallons once a year removes most of it. Turn off the power or gas, let the water cool, attach a garden hose to the drain valve, and flush until the water runs clear. The U.S. Department of Energy notes that regular maintenance keeps water heaters running efficiently for their full expected lifespan of 8–12 years. ## 4. Never pour grease down the drain

This is the most violated rule in home plumbing. Cooking grease looks harmless as a liquid, but it solidifies inside your pipes and catches everything that follows. Over months, it builds into a blockage that no amount of hot water will clear. Pour used oil into a can, let it harden, and throw it in the trash. For small amounts, wipe pans with a paper towel before washing.

## 5. Use drain strainers everywhere

Hair is the number one cause of bathroom drain clogs, and food particles top the list in kitchens. A two-dollar mesh strainer over every drain catches both before they become a problem. Clean the strainers weekly — it takes seconds and eliminates the most common reason people call a plumber.

## 6. Fix running toilets promptly

A toilet that runs constantly can waste up to 200 gallons of water per day, according to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense program. In most cases the culprit is a worn flapper that costs less than ten dollars and takes fifteen minutes to replace. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank; if color appears in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

## 7. Be careful what you flush

Toilets are designed for exactly two things. "Flushable" wipes are the industry's most profitable fiction — they do not break down like toilet paper and are a leading cause of sewer blockages worldwide. Dental floss, cotton swabs, and paper towels belong in the trash, not the bowl. One bad flush can back up an entire household.

## 8. Watch your water pressure

High water pressure feels great in the shower, but anything above 80 psi stresses pipes, joints, appliance valves, and water heaters. You can check it with a ten-dollar gauge screwed onto an outdoor spigot. If it reads high, a pressure-reducing valve installed where the main line enters the house protects your entire system. Many municipal supplies run hot, so this check is worth doing at least once.

## 9. Insulate exposed pipes before winter

Frozen pipes burst, and burst pipes flood. Any pipe running through an unheated garage, crawl space, attic, or exterior wall is at risk. Foam pipe sleeves cost very little and slide on in minutes. On the coldest nights, letting faucets drip slightly keeps water moving, which makes freezing far less likely. If you travel in winter, never set the thermostat below 55°F (13°C).

## 10. Schedule a professional inspection every couple of years

There is a limit to what eyes and hands can find. A licensed plumber with a camera can inspect your main sewer line — the most expensive pipe in your home to replace — and spot root intrusion or bellies before they collapse. Think of it like a dental checkup: slightly annoying, far cheaper than the alternative. Ask for a written report with photos so you can track changes over time.

## The bottom line

Plumbing maintenance is not glamorous, but it follows a simple economic law: every dollar of prevention saves many dollars of repair. Work through this list once, put the recurring items on your calendar, and your plumbing system will quietly do its job for decades — which is exactly what you want from pipes you never have to think about.
